There's a new explanation circulating about why mini PCs can sometimes run large 70-billion parameter AI models that big GPUs can't. It's all about unified memory, right?

REPORTER: That's right. The article "Unified Memory, Explained" sheds light on a crucial architectural difference. Traditional PCs and many dedicated AI GPUs have separate pools of RAM for the CPU and VRAM for the GPU. This means large models often have to shuttle data back and forth, creating bottlenecks. Mini PCs, especially those with integrated graphics and unified memory architectures, share a single pool of high-bandwidth RAM between the CPU and GPU. While this shared memory might be slower than dedicated VRAM in some aspects, its sheer size – often much larger than typical GPU VRAM – allows these mini PCs to load and run very large language models, like those with 70 billion parameters, that simply wouldn't fit into the limited VRAM of many powerful discrete GPUs. The trade-off comes in inference speed, but the ability to run these models locally is a game-changer for accessibility and offline AI.
