REPORTER: This 1978 essay, authored by the renowned computer scientist Edsger W. Dijkstra, offers a prescient critique of "natural language programming." Dijkstra argued that attempting to program computers using everyday human language is inherently flawed and foolish. His central point was that natural languages are ambiguous, imprecise, and laden with context-dependent meanings, which stand in stark contrast to the unambiguous, formal logic required for computer execution. He believed that the discipline of programming lay precisely in learning to think in a structured, precise, and logical manner – a discipline that would be undermined by the illusion of effortless natural language interaction. This historical perspective remains remarkably relevant today as we grapple with large language models and attempts to create more "natural" interfaces, highlighting the enduring tension between human expressiveness and computational rigor.
